LESSON 01 OF 09

Meet the letters

حروف کی پہچان

Read from right to left. Notice the main shape and the number and position of dots. Learn a letter’s name first, then practise its sound with your teacher.

Point to baa, taa and thaa. What changes between their shapes? Say each name with a teacher.

LESSON 02 OF 09

Look at joined shapes

ملے ہوئے حروف

Most letters change shape when joined. Read the letters in each group from right to left. Alif, daal, dhaal, raa, zaay and waaw do not connect to the following letter on their left.

بSeparate baa
بـInitial baa
ـبـMiddle baa
ـبFinal baa
بتBaa + taa
تمTaa + meem
لمLaam + meem
منMeem + noon
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Trace the baa shapes. Find the dot below in every position. These are letter combinations for practice.

LESSON 03 OF 09

Fathah · zabar

زبر

Fathah is the small stroke above a letter. It gives a short “a” vowel. Keep the sound short; do not turn it into a long vowel.

بَba
تَta
جَja
دَda
سَsa
مَma
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Read the six combinations slowly. Ask your teacher to check the letter sound and the short vowel.

LESSON 04 OF 09

Kasrah · zer

زیر

Kasrah is the small stroke below a letter. It gives a short “i” vowel. Compare the same letter with fathah and kasrah.

بِbi
تِti
جِji
دِdi
سِsi
مِmi
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Alternate بَ and بِ. Keep the consonant the same while changing only the vowel.

LESSON 05 OF 09

Dammah · pesh

پیش

Dammah is the small curled mark above a letter. It gives a short “u” vowel. Practise a clear short sound rather than stretching it.

بُbu
تُtu
جُju
دُdu
سُsu
مُmu
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Read بَ بِ بُ, then مَ مِ مُ. Your teacher can model each sound before you repeat.

LESSON 06 OF 09

Tanween

تنوین

Tanween uses doubled vowel marks and adds a final noon sound in connected reading: an, in or un. Stopping at the end of a word has additional rules; practise those with a teacher.

بًban
بٍbin
بٌbun
تًtan
تٍtin
تٌtun
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Compare a single vowel with its doubled mark. Listen for the added noon sound. These are sound drills, not Quran verses.

LESSON 07 OF 09

Sukoon and blending

سکون

Sukoon marks a letter without its own vowel. Join it to the sound before it without adding an extra vowel. The mouth position matters as much as the written mark.

بَتْbat
مِنْmin
قُلْqul
لَمْlam
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Identify the letter carrying sukoon, then blend the combination as one unit. Ask a teacher to check the final consonant.

LESSON 08 OF 09

Madd · long vowels

مد

A basic long vowel is held for two counts. Alif follows fathah; a saakin waaw follows dammah; a saakin yaa follows kasrah. Other madd rules depend on what follows.

بَاbaa
بُوboo
بِيbee
تَاtaa
تُوtoo
تِيtee
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Compare بَ with بَا. Keep an even two-count length for the basic long vowel.

LESSON 09 OF 09

Shaddah and revision

تشدید اور دہرائی

Shaddah marks a doubled consonant: the first part closes the preceding sound and the second part carries the written vowel. Noon and meem with shaddah also have ghunnah, taught in Tajweed.

بَّDoubled baa
تَّDoubled taa
رَّDoubled raa
مَّMeem with shaddah
نَّNoon with shaddah
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Name the marks in بَ بِ بُ بْ بَّ. Revisit any mark you cannot yet identify, then move to short Quran passages with your teacher.
