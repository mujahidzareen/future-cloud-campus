LESSON 01 OF 08

Makharij · where sounds begin

مخارج

Articulation is the place where a letter’s sound is formed. Begin with the throat, tongue and lips, and let a teacher demonstrate differences that transliteration cannot represent.

ء هThroat-letter practice
ع حThroat-letter practice
غ خThroat-letter practice
ب مLip-letter comparison
ق كBack-of-tongue comparison
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Listen to one pair, repeat it, then ask your teacher to check the sound. A written guide cannot assess pronunciation.

LESSON 02 OF 08

Ghunnah

غنہ

Ghunnah is a nasal sound. A noon or meem carrying shaddah is held with ghunnah for two counts. Keep the sound steady rather than adding a separate vowel.

نّNoon mushaddadah
مّMeem mushaddadah
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Find the doubled noon or meem in your reading. Let a teacher model two even counts.

LESSON 03 OF 08

Izhar · pronounce clearly

اظہار

For noon saakin or tanween followed by one of the six throat letters, pronounce the noon sound clearly. Keep the following letter distinct.

ء ه ع ح غ خSix izhar letters
نْ + ءNoon saakin + hamzah
نْ + عNoon saakin + ayn
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Identify the letter after noon saakin. If it is a throat letter, practise a clear separation with your teacher.

LESSON 04 OF 08

Idgham · merge sounds

ادغام

In the usual cross-word rule, noon saakin or tanween merges into a following ي ن م و ل ر. With ي ن م و there is ghunnah; with ل ر there is no ghunnah. There are exceptions, including certain within-word combinations.

ي ن م وWith ghunnah
ل رWithout ghunnah
نْ + مCross-word letter drill
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Sort the six letters into the two groups. Ask your teacher to demonstrate the difference in connected reading.

LESSON 05 OF 08

Iqlab · change the sound

اقلاب

When noon saakin or tanween is followed by baa, the noon sound changes to a concealed meem sound with ghunnah. In many mushafs a small meem helps mark the rule.

نْ + بNoon saakin before baa
بThe iqlab trigger letter
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Spot baa after noon saakin or tanween. Practise the transition slowly with a teacher rather than pronouncing a full clear noon.

LESSON 06 OF 08

Ikhfa · conceal the noon

اخفاء

For the remaining fifteen letters, noon saakin or tanween is concealed with ghunnah. The tongue prepares for the next letter; the sound is between clear izhar and full merging.

ت ث ج د ذ ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ف ق كFifteen ikhfa letters
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Choose one trigger letter and ask your teacher to model it. Repeat without making a complete clear noon or doubling the next consonant.

LESSON 07 OF 08

Qalqalah

قلقلہ

When one of ق ط ب ج د is saakin, including when stopping makes it saakin, release the consonant with a controlled echo. Do not add a new vowel after it.

ق ط ب ج دQalqalah letters
قْQaaf with sukoon
طْTaa with sukoon
بْBaa with sukoon
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Name the five letters, then compare a saakin letter with a vowelled letter under a teacher’s guidance.

LESSON 08 OF 08

Madd and stopping

مد اور وقف

Basic madd is two counts. Longer madd depends on its cause and the reading being followed. Stopping signs guide where to pause; breath, word endings and meaning also need guided practice.

ا و يBasic madd letters
مـCompulsory-stop sign
لاDo not deliberately stop here
جPermissible-stop sign
YOUR PRACTICE

Try it with your teacher.

Read a short passage with your teacher. Mark a suitable stopping point and check what happens to the last sound.
