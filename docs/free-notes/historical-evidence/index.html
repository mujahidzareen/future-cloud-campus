Evidence and inference in history

A source can tell you something directly and suggest further possibilities. Separate what it shows from what you infer.

History · Secondary

Core ideas

  1. Identify the author or creator, date, audience and purpose where available.
  2. Describe relevant evidence before drawing an inference.
  3. Compare with other evidence. A source's usefulness depends on the question being asked.
WORKED EXAMPLE

A practice source is a fictional factory poster asking workers to increase output. It is direct evidence of the message the poster presents. It cannot, by itself, prove that every worker agreed with the message.

Try it yourself

Attempt each question before opening the answer.

1. What is an inference?

An interpretation drawn from evidence.

2. Can one poster establish what every person believed?

No. More evidence is needed.

3. Why consider the intended audience?

It helps explain how and why the message was presented in that form.
